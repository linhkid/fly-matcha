## Leg motor pools: which muscle, which joint, which way

**Leg motor pool to joint mapping for MaleCNS v1.0, from the literature**

Pandas check (381 leg motor neurons, 373 typed):
- The 16 other type names also appear in ml and hl; these three are front-leg only.
- Front-leg only: "Ta depressor MN", "Ta levator MN" and "Tergopleural/Pleural promotor MN". Cheong et al. could not find serial homologs for them in T2/T3.
- In their place, ml has 24 neurons under systematic names with no muscle assignment: MNml29 and MNml76–83.
- hl has 21 such neurons: MNhl01, 02, 29, 59, 60, 62, 64, 65, 87 and 88. Six of them exit AbN1.
- 8 rows have no type (6 hl, 2 fl).
- Tergotr. MN counts are 8 fl, 2 ml and 2 hl.
- The giant T2 jump neuron is a separate type (TTMn, 2 cells, plus STTMm, 4 cells). Its subclass is wm, so it is not among these leg motor neurons.

Each line reads: type | muscle and where it sits | joint | what it does | confidence [source].

- Tergopleural/Pleural promotor | two promotor muscles in the thorax | body-coxa | swings the whole leg forward, active at the stance-to-swing transition | high [L, O, C]
- Pleural remotor/abductor | thorax | body-coxa | pulls the leg back and outward, active in stance to propel the body | high [C, O]
- Sternal anterior rotator | thorax | body-coxa | rotates the leg forward during swing | high [C]
- Sternal posterior rotator | thorax | body-coxa | rotates the leg backward during stance | high [C]
- Sternal adductor | thorax | body-coxa | pulls the coxa toward the midline; the direction comes from the name. | medium [C]
  - Cheong groups it with the anterior rotator as a pool opposing another coxa movement.
- Tergotr. | runs from dorsal thorax cuticle across body-coxa to the trochanter | coxa-trochanter | extends the trochanter, which LOWERS the leg and pushes it down | high [L, C]
- Sternotrochanter | runs from ventral thorax cuticle to the trochanter | coxa-trochanter | extends, which LOWERS the leg | high [L]
- Tr extensor | coxa (not confirmed verbatim) | coxa-trochanter | extends, which LOWERS the femur and pushes the foot down | high [L, A]
- Tr flexor | coxa | coxa-trochanter | flexes, which LIFTS the femur | high [F, A]
  - Feng: "femur elevation (flexion of the coxa-trochanter joint)".
  - Azevedo: giant-fibre input drives T1 trochanter and tibia flexors, giving front-leg lift-off.
- Acc. tr flexor | a separate muscle on the same joint; its segment was not verified | coxa-trochanter | LIFTS | medium [L]
- Fe reductor | in the trochanter | trochanter-femur, a nearly fused joint | function unknown, so do not drive a joint with this pool | high [A]
- Ti extensor (SETi and FETi) | femur | femur-tibia | straightens the knee | high [A, C]
- Ti flexor | femur | femur-tibia | bends the knee | high [A, L, F]
- Acc. ti flexor | second flexor muscle in the femur | femur-tibia | bends the knee | medium-high [L]
- Ta depressor | tibia | tibia-tarsus | pushes the foot down; direction is from the name only | medium [L]
- Ta levator | tibia | tibia-tarsus | raises the foot; direction is from the name only | medium [A, L]
- ltm2-femur | fibres in the femur insert on the long tendon | claw | controls the claw (the tendon is the claw retractor) | high for target, medium for movement [A, L]
- ltm1-tibia | fibres in the tibia insert on the same tendon | claw | same as ltm2-femur | same
- ltm | long tendon muscle; Azevedo could not resolve whether these smaller neurons go to the femur or tibia part | claw | same as ltm2-femur | medium [A]

Caveats
1. The front leg runs opposite to the hind leg in forward walking: its coxa-trochanter and femur-tibia joints flex in stance (pulling the body) and extend in swing (Feng). "Flexor = swing" is wrong for T1.
2. Azevedo's swing/stance colour coding is in a figure only, so I could not read it as text. Only the pools where Cheong or Özdil state a phase carry one above.
3. T2/T3 assignments are serial-homology inferences from T1 (Cheong), so treat ml/hl as one step less certain.
4. I could not open Soler 2004, Baek & Mann 2009, Brierley 2012, Marin 2024 or Azevedo's motor-neuron appendix. The Soler-to-Azevedo name equivalences (levator/depressor, and "tibia reductor" = Acc. ti flexor?) are unverified.
5. Motor-neuron counts per muscle differ between papers (for example 6 vs 8 Tr flexor MNs in FANC). I did not reconcile these with the MaleCNS counts.
6. None of these sources model tiredness, and I did not research fly fatigue.

## Movement senses, command neurons, and what a static-weight model can show

Tiredness (user's question)
- No, he cannot tire. The model's entire state is v, g and a 2.2 ms refractory counter (`/Users/linhkid/Documents/Fun/fly-matcha/lab/flylab/model/oracle.py`), and all of it decays to zero within about 100 ms. Minute 10 of brewing is statistically the same as second 1.
- Real flies tire through receptor adaptation, synaptic depression, neuromodulation, muscle metabolism and sleep pressure. None of these is in a connectome LIF (rule 7). Any visible fatigue would be staged and must say so.

Proprioceptors
- The femoral chordotonal organ (FeCO) has about 150 cells per leg. Claw neurons give a tonic signal of tibia position, with separate flexed and extended subtypes and hysteresis. Hook neurons are phasic and signal movement direction. Club neurons signal movement in either direction plus vibration of roughly 0.2–2 kHz at very small amplitude (Mamiya 2018, 2023).
- Hair plates are tonic detectors of joint limits (Pratt 2026).
- Campaniform sensilla sense load and cuticle strain. A suspended or cartoon leg has no load, so do not drive them from kinematics.
- Claw and hook pathways feed local motor circuits. Club pathways are pooled across legs and ascend to the brain (Agrawal 2020; Lee 2025).
- Ascending neurons mostly carry behavioural state (walking, resting, grooming) to AVLP/GNG, not raw joint angles (Chen 2023).
- In real flies, hook axons are presynaptically inhibited during self-generated walking and grooming, while position-encoding axons are not (Dallmann 2025). A static-weight model cannot gate this, so hook drive during "his own" movement will be too strong.
- Firing rates: the Drosophila FeCO data are calcium imaging only, and I found no published spike rates, so any Hz mapping is model, not fact.
  - Anchors: locust FeCO afferents fire tonically with joint angle and phasically with velocity (Usherwood 1968), and Pugliese caps model rates at about 200 Hz.
  - My suggested mapping, not from any paper: claw 0–about 100 Hz with angle, hook 0–about 150 Hz with signed velocity, club brief bursts.
- The annotation table only partly separates claw, hook and club:
  - The `synonyms` column labels just 33 neurons (claw 13, club 15, hook 5), all in MetaLN, which is hind legs only.
  - Claw is mostly type SNpp50. Hook is SNpp39/41. Club is spread over SNpp40/47/60/56/57.
  - Mid and front legs have to be inferred by type, and that inference is ours, not the table's.
  - SNpp52/45 are hair plates, SNpp53 is campaniform, and SApp23 is ascending chordotonal.
  - 78 neurons are untyped (SNppxx) and 66 have no type at all.

Descending neurons
- DNp09 (P9) drives forward walking with ipsilateral turning (Bidaye 2020), but activation also causes freezing (Zacarias 2018). Headless flies do not walk when it is activated; it needs 32 downstream DNs (Braun 2024). Same neuron as in the papers. Medium confidence.
- DNa02 shortens ipsilateral strides and drives fast turns. Evidence is activation plus recording (Rayshubskiy; Yang 2024). Same neuron. High confidence.
- DNa01 activity correlates with slow, sustained steering. No clean activation phenotype. Medium-low.
- DNa03 is upstream of DNa02 (Yang 2024). Evidence is connectome-led. Low.
- DNg13 lengthens contralateral strides (Yang 2024). High. The table flags it as sexually dimorphic.
- DNb02: I found no behavioural paper. The table gives it subclass upper tectulum and glutamate, so it is not a leg command neuron. Skip it.
- MDN (synonym DNp50, 4 neurons) drives backward walking, and this survives decapitation (Bidaye 2014; Feng 2020; Braun 2024). Highest confidence, and the best choice for route 2.
- DNg100 (BDN2) and DNg97 (oDN1) initiate forward walking (Sapkal 2024). Medium-high. DNg100 is the top rhythm driver in Pugliese 2025.
- DNp07 and DNp10 drive landing leg extension in flight and takeoff when standing (Ache 2019). High confidence, but the effect depends on behavioural state.
- DNg62 (aDN1) and DNge078 (aDN2, MANC type DNfl023) drive antennal grooming (Hampel 2015). aDN2 alone in headless flies gives only a front-leg approach. High. Name collision: "aDN" in this table also labels the fruitless-cluster type AOTU103m.
- DNg12: Guo 2022 used a driver-line population, and activation gave leg rubbing alternating with ventral head sweeps.
  - MaleCNS splits DNg12 into _a to _h, 41 neurons in total.
  - Only DNg12_e (6 neurons, front-leg subclass) maps to MANC "DNg12". The others map to neck or other targets (DNnt*, DNxn*).
  - Which subtypes Guo's driver line covered is unknown. Medium-low.

What the model can and cannot show
- It can show which motor neurons and interneurons a DN or a proprioceptor class recruits, on which side and in which segment, against a control. That is wiring reach with staged drive.
- It cannot show movement, force, state gating (flight versus standing, the P9 freeze-or-walk split), presynaptic gating, neuromodulation or fatigue.
- Shiu 2024 modelled the brain only and read out DN and motor-neuron firing. It has no VNC, no rhythm and no baseline activity, so it cannot show inhibition of silent cells.
- Rhythm is not impossible with static weights.
  - Pugliese 2025 applied tonic DNg100 drive to MANC, with static weights and no intrinsic bursting, and got 7–15 Hz motor-neuron oscillation.
  - The source is a recurrent E–E–I triplet: IN17A001, INXXX466 and IN16B036.
  - They report it replicated in a LIF model and in the male CNS connectome.
  - All three types are in our table, six neurons each, one per leg neuromere.
- Caveats on that result:
  - It is a preprint.
  - Only about 3% of DNs gave rhythm.
  - They randomised parameters instead of tuning them.
  - It produced no inter-leg coordination.
- For us this is a pre-registered experiment with a control, and failure is a result.

## The six taste neurons the dataset calls unclear

**What the six are**
- Verified in the feather tables: LB1b is 6 neurons (3 L, 3 R), class gustatory, subclass labellar bristle, entry nerve MxLbN. receptorType and ground_truth are empty.
- The per-neuron calls are as you stated. The two serotonin top calls in LB1b are the only serotonin calls among all 1,416 typed gustatory neurons.
- The taste-feeding connectome paper (Tastekin … Jefferis, Ribeiro; bioRxiv 2025, now Cell) matched Gr33a-GAL4, the pan-bitter line, to LB1a–d as a group, using midline-ring morphology. I did not read this in full: the search excerpts agree with the project's RESEARCH.md, but every direct fetch of the paper returned 403.
- The paper says the four subtypes are consistent with the four known bitter bristle classes (S-a, S-b, I-a, I-b).
- I found no statement of which class or receptor LB1b is. It is bitter by group match only, not by recording.
- LB1a scores high with proboscis motor neurons; LB1b–e score low.

**Transmitter of taste neurons**
- ChAT immunostaining labels subsets of gustatory afferents (Yasuyama & Salvaterra 1999; Python & Stocker 2002, larva). I read these from abstracts only.
- Jaeger et al. 2018 (eLife), read directly: VGlut is not expressed in bitter (Gr66a), sweet or water GRNs. The S-a bristles have a Gr66a+ Ppk23+ GRN that is ChAT-positive. The only glutamatergic GRNs are the Ppk23glut cells.
- This is direct evidence for a subset of bitter GRNs. For the rest it is inferred by class; I found no study showing ChAT in every bitter GRN.
- Serotonin in a GRN: I found no report. In the larva, serotonin fibres come from a central neuron, not from afferents. I judge a serotonergic GRN implausible.

**Why the dataset says unclear**
- Rule inferred from the table, not from documentation (the MaleCNS preprint and Nern 2025 methods were blocked):
  - Every clear neuron-level call has at least 50 predictions and confidence at or above 0.50.
  - Type-level calls below 0.50 become unclear. LB1b is at 0.49.
  - Serotonin and octopamine calls without ground truth are demoted to unclear in the consensus (297 of 386 serotonin rows).
  - From a search excerpt of Nern et al. 2025: low-confidence types are reported as unclear unless confirmed experimentally, and monoamine calls are unreliable.
- Eckstein et al. 2024, read directly:
  - Many first-order sensory neurons were mispredicted as serotonin instead of acetylcholine in both datasets.
  - Serotonin was the least reliable class (33–38%).
- No gustatory neuron has ground truth. Sensory ground truth covers only olfactory, visual and 16 proprioceptive neurons.
- Unclear rates among typed neurons:

  | Group | Unclear | Rate |
  |---|---|---|
  | Gustatory | 259 of 1,416 | 18.3% |
  | Non-sensory | — | 1.4% |
  | Labellar bristle | 13 of 161 | 8.1% |

- Inference: LB1b's unclear label is a known classifier weak spot (acetylcholine votes diluted by spurious serotonin votes). It is not evidence of a different transmitter.

**Shiu et al. 2024**
- Read directly: there is no unknown category. Each synapse takes its top prediction, and a neuron is inhibitory only if more than half of its synapses are GABA or glutamate.
- Everything else, including dopamine, serotonin and octopamine, is treated as excitatory.
- Under that rule LB1b would be +1.

**Recommendation**
- Test one variant: gustatory sensory neurons with consensus "unclear" take sign +1.
- It changes 259 neurons: LB1b 6, LB2b 3, LB2d 5, LB3 1, LgLG1b 134, LgLG8 14, WG3 96.
- Reasons:
  - Sibling types LB1a, LB1c and LB1d share the same line and nerve and are acetylcholine at 0.64–0.71.
  - There is primary ChAT evidence in bitter GRNs.
  - The classifier's failure mode is documented.
  - The rule is narrower than "every unknown neuron gets +1", which would also flip motor, endocrine and amine cells.
- What would make the variant wrong:
  - LB1b turns out to be glutamatergic like Ppk23glut or LB3d. No LB1b neuron has glutamate as its top call, and Gr66a cells lack VGlut, so this is unlikely for LB1b.
  - A labellar ChAT or scRNA intersection finds a Gr33a cell without ChAT.
  - A later release labels LB1b as something other than acetylcholine.
  - Caveat: WG3 and LgLG1b carry many glutamate calls, so the variant is weakest there. Report leg and wing effects separately.

**On the user's tiredness question**
Transmitter sign does not bear on it. RESEARCH.md records that nothing in the model outlasts about 50 ms, so no fatigue or adaptation can emerge.
