Census: the taste circuit

Who plays which part when a male fly tastes sweet and bitter, and the populations the branches will need. Every binding carries its evidence. Roles marked required gate slice 01.

Dataset MaleCNS v1.0 (Janelia FlyEM, CC-BY 4.0) · annotations sha256 2177e246113e4cfb… · 211,577 annotated bodies, 164,506 with a type · A lock was written.

Census gate: PASS. Every required role is bound on independent evidence and every anchor is accounted for.

Notes

Why another project counted 165 labellar sugar neurons

There are exactly 165 labellar bristle taste neurons in MaleCNS, across 16 types and every modality: bitter, sweet, water, salt and unassigned. The flyverse-core project's '165 labellar sugar GRNs' is that whole population. By the paper's assignment the sweet ones are LB3b and LB3c, 34 neurons.

Some taste neurons are mute in the base model

15 of the 165 have no usable transmitter label ('unclear' in the source): all of LB1b (6), LB2b (3), LB2d (5) and the single LB3. Under the base sign policy an unlabelled neuron's outputs carry sign 0. Slice 05 runs a second arm with sign +1, and the difference for bitter is these six LB1b neurons.

No taste neuron and no named taste relay is flagged as dimorphic

The dimorphism column is empty for every gustatory sensory neuron and for all 43 neurons cited to Shiu 2022. That is the dataset's headline, the periphery is the same in both sexes, seen on this circuit. Branch B6 turns it into the maleness strip.

Johnston's organ types use hyphens

JO-FV, JO-CM, JO-EV1 and so on, 672 neurons in 34 types. 'JO-C' alone is not a type, which is why the explorer returned nothing for it.
